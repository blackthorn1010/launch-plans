## SUMMARY OF WHAT CHANGED

1. Targeting moved from a fixed name roster to capability scoring, and
   both sides of the market are now recruited simultaneously.
2. Cemeteries and vault companies are peer categories, not deferred.
3. Vendor credentialing is attestation-based; burden of proof sits
   entirely with the vendor.
4. A retention layer was added to the build, because nothing shipped
   currently earns a second post once the home has the vendor's number.
5. Kill gates were re-cut to measure the right failure.
6. A year-end records layer and a job-attached forms library were added
   to the build. Settling on TradeBook removes 1099-NEC paperwork the
   home would otherwise owe — that is now a stated part of the pitch.
7. Money, brand split, hard gates, sit-down script, and channels are
   unchanged.

## SECTION A — EDITS TO ULTIMATE.md

### A1. THE ONE RULE — replace
- **Was:** one metro with 8 homes posting and 6 vendors bidding beats 80
  accounts across three states.
- **Now:** one city where 3 homes can post and 4 vendors can fill beats
  30 accounts with nothing that pairs. Capability first, density second,
  ads third, App Store last.
- **Why:** density counted in accounts hid the fact that one side alone
  is worth nothing.

### A2. POSITION — replace the category ladder
- **Was:** "Vault / retort / cooler / grounds wait until removals award
  weekly."
- **Now:** all categories live at launch — removal/transport, trade
  embalming, cemetery services (open/close, vault setting, monument
  setting, grounds), vault supply and delivery, crematory, equipment.
  Removals stay the *pitch* because they're the 2 a.m. pain, but no
  category is held back on the roster.
- **Why:** a cemetery both posts work and fills it, so one account is
  two-sided density in a single signature — no funeral home does that.
  Cemetery work is also daylight and plannable, which keeps the board
  from looking dead at noon.

### A3. FIRST 10 — REAL NAMES — delete, replace with scoring
There are no confirmed homes and no confirmed vendors. The v1 roster was
a candidate list being read as a commitment.
- Replace with the sourcing pass and scoring rubrics in
  `ULTIMATE-v2-TARGETING.md` (25 homes and 20 vendors scored, top 12 of
  each contacted).
- Home target profile: **high volume, thin staff** — roughly 100–250
  cases with one or two employees. Under ~40 cases doesn't generate
  enough overflow to build a habit; 400+ has its own vans. Both extremes
  score zero deliberately.
- Vendor scoring leads with 2 a.m. coverage. A vendor who can't answer
  at night can't serve the overflow pitch, which is the whole product.
- Obituary counts over the last 90 days are the free case-volume proxy.
- Remove Elmwood and Jefferson Memorial from the sit-down list — v1 both
  deferred cemeteries *and* listed two of them, and flagged Elmwood as
  script-only in the same document.
- No name enters a script until a human verifies it. Do not invent.

### A4. TWO LISTS — replace with the pairing rule
- Never let one side get more than **2 net ahead** in a city.
- Weekly cadence, Birmingham only, weeks 1–8: 2 home sit-downs (in
  person, on repair days) + 6 vendor calls (phone, from the truck).
  They don't compete for the same hours, which is why they run together.
- Every home sit-down ends with: *"When you farm one out now, who do
  you call?"* Every vendor call ends with: *"Which homes do you already
  run for?"* That is how the list builds itself.

### A5. GEOGRAPHY — tiering
1. Birmingham / Jefferson (~6,500–7,500 deaths) — everything starts here.
2. Huntsville / Madison (~3,818) — opens only on 3 real awards, 2+ bids
   per job, and 3 vendors who have bid and lost. Not a calendar date.
3. Montgomery — later.
- Jasper / Cullman / Walker — **deprioritized.** Serve as dropoffs on a
  Birmingham job; do not recruit there.

### A6. KILL GATES — re-cut
- **Week 2, supply gate (new):** fewer than 3 reachable, willing removal
  vendors in Birmingham → stop home sit-downs entirely, spend the week
  on the phone. A home burned on an unfillable job will not sit down
  twice.
- **Week 4, post gate (replaces v1 week 3):** zero real posts by anyone
  but you *and* fewer than 10 known outsourcing opportunities across
  onboarded homes. Get the denominator by asking every home to text you
  when they next farm one out, even if they don't use the book. Four to
  six comped homes only generate 8–12 chances in three weeks, all at
  2 a.m. — the v1 gate would have read a recall failure as a product
  failure.
- **Week 8, award gate:** unchanged. Under 8 real awards total, or never
  3 in one week.

### A7. SCOREBOARD — add
Pairing health: matched pairs in tier-1 city · home:vendor balance ·
vendors who actually answered at 2 a.m. (tested).
Leakage: repeat-pair share · same-vendor re-post rate · handshake vs
Pay-on-TradeBook. Standing rule: handshake over 40% after award #10 →
add record value, do not raise the fee.
Counts: add **first paid seat (dated)**. Pull one conversion forward to
around week 8 — one home who has actually used the book, at the annual
rate. Otherwise the first invoice lands *after* the week-12 decision and
you'll decide on award volume with zero evidence anyone will pay.

### A8. RECUSE-OR-PUBLISH — resolve now, not later
Phase 1 *is* an equipment award (home posts a down Portiboy, you bid it,
week one). Recusal deletes Phase 1, so the decision is already made:
**publish.** Write the founder-vendor disclosure, put it on vendor
signup and in the terms, and pair it with sealed bids enforced
server-side and a named third party — not you — who decides any dispute
on a job you bid.

### A9. SCRIPTS — add cold versions
v1's scripts assumed an anchor vouching for you. There isn't one yet.
Cold vendor and cold home scripts are in `ULTIMATE-v2-TARGETING.md`.
Never promise volume you don't have; a vendor who was oversold doesn't
answer the second call.

### A11. BRAND SPLIT — amend for the forms library
The locked rule says Gumroad kits are Service Ops and stay off
tradebook.co. Putting the kits into the app would contradict it. The
resolution that keeps both:
- **In-app forms are TradeBook's** — unbranded, job-attached, no Service
  Ops mark, no Standridge mark, no Gumroad link, no cross-sell.
- **Service Ops keeps selling the standalone kits on Gumroad** to people
  who aren't on the book. Different product, different buyer.
You are not moving your kits into TradeBook; you are using what you
already know about these forms to build TradeBook's document layer.
Write that sentence into the brand section so it doesn't drift.

### A12. UNCHANGED — do not edit
MONEY (dual $29 seats, fee $0 on awards 1–10 then 10% on top / $15 min /
$150 cap, handshake only after a first Pay-on-TradeBook charge and never
refunding a fee), BRAND SPLIT, HARD GATES, the 10-minute sit-down script
with its PASS/FAIL, CHANNELS, WHAT WE THREW AWAY, YEAR 1 MONEY.

## SECTION B — BUILD BACKLOG FOR GROK BUILD

Full detail in `TRADEBOOK-RETENTION-SPEC.md`. Order matters.

**B1. Job record schema + sealed bids.** Everything else writes into it.
Job ID `TB-YYYYMM-####`, posted-by, bids (price/ETA/method/timestamp),
award, change orders, completion, settlement, amounts, payout. Sealed
bids enforced **server-side** — no vendor sees another's price, count,
or identity, TJ included. A bid list leaking through an API response is
the fastest way to lose the founding 25.

**B2. Same-vendor re-post.** From any completed job: one tap, prefilled
job type, locations, method, instructions, requested-vendor chip, and
last award price shown as reference only. Never prefill the *time* —
that gets re-entered every post. Entry points: job detail, Post tab
("Repeat a past job"), vendor profile.

**B3. Requested vendor + 30-minute head start.** Requested vendor gets
push and SMS first and is the only one who can see the job during the
window, with a live countdown on the home's screen. Decline or expiry
opens it to the corridor automatically: *"Stateline hasn't picked up.
Now open to 5 corridor vendors."* Home can open it early. Do **not**
auto-award at last price. This mechanic is the pitch made literal — use
your guy, but you're not stuck if he doesn't answer — and a phone call
cannot do the fallback.

**B4. Receipt PDF.** Generated at completion, permanent, both sides.
Job ID, orgs, all timestamps, winning bid detail, change orders with
reason and approver, totals, fee, payout, settlement method. Footer:
TradeBook is a booking record, not a funeral establishment, not a
carrier, takes no custody of remains.

**B5. Change orders.** Vendor requests on the live job — extra man,
extra miles, holdover, look-then-repair became repair, access/stairs.
Home sees one card: Approve / Decline / Call. Fee recalculates on the
**approved** total, never the requested one, still min $15 / cap $150.
If the extra $80 happens by text you lose the fee and the record.

**B6. Vendor credentialing — attestation model.** One screen, under 90
seconds, no uploads, no verification gate, no pending state:
- business name, contact, counties, categories, 24/7 yes/no
- checkboxes: holds required licenses · carries GL and commercial auto ·
  BBP training current where required · **will produce proof on request
  including at the job site** · will notify TradeBook if any stops being
  true
- one typed license number for trade embalming and cremation only
  (AL licenses embalmers and cremationists individually). Optional field
  for removals pending the Board answer in C1. No license field at all
  for cemetery, vault, or equipment — those are insurance-and-attestation
  only, which is what the law actually supports.
- typed signature, timestamp, IP.

**B7. On-site credential check, logged to the job.** Home taps
`Proof shown` / `Not asked` / `Could not produce`. Writes to the record,
prints on the receipt. This is what makes the attestation worth
anything — the home gets "verified on site at 2:14 a.m., logged" instead
of a checkbox from eight months ago, and you get vendor quality data
without doing verification work.
Display `Could not produce` as **the home's finding**, never as
TradeBook's determination.

**B8. Bid card language.** `Licensed & insured — self-attested · Shows
proof on site · 4 jobs on TradeBook`. Tap explains in one sentence that
TradeBook does not verify credentials. Never let a screen imply
otherwise — that is the only way TradeBook takes on burden it doesn't
have.

**B9. SMS-to-post.** Home texts the Quo line, gets a prefilled link, one
tap to post. If untapped in 10 minutes it alerts TJ to post on their
behalf. The 2 a.m. failure is memory, not product; this is also what
makes the week-4 gate measure the product. Build after the Quo line is
live.

**B10. Leakage metrics** feeding the Monday scoreboard, computed not
self-reported: repeat-pair share, same-vendor re-post rate, handshake
share, requested-vendor fallback rate, head-start conversion.

**B11. Year-end statement — not a tax form, no filing obligation.**
Per counterparty per year: job count, gross, change orders, fees, net,
split by Pay-on-TradeBook vs handshake, with the job list. CSV + PDF,
both sides. On the **home's** version, a separate section listing
vendors they settled with **directly**, totalled and flagged past the
current 1099-NEC threshold, with one sentence saying it may require
them to issue a 1099-NEC and to confirm with their accountant. Genuine
service and a quiet argument for keeping the next one on the book. It
must not scold. Ship this year; it carries no filing duty.

**B12. W-9 at vendor onboarding**, not at year end — you need it for
payouts anyway, and a vendor who's gone by December won't send one.
Show `W-9 on file` on the vendor's own account screen.

**B13. Settlement-screen copy.** At the moment a home chooses handshake,
show the trade plainly: pay on the book and the processor reports it,
settle direct and past the threshold it's their W-9, their 1099-NEC,
their January. Say **may** owe, never **will** — payments to
corporations are generally exempt (with carve-outs), though most small
corridor operators are sole props or single-member LLCs and are
reportable.

**B14. Forms library — attached to jobs, never a Documents tab.** Every
form hangs off a `job_id`, appears on the job screen when it's needed,
prints into that job's receipt, and rolls into the year-end statement.
The record layer and the forms library are one feature. Build order:
1. **Transfer of custody receipt** (removal) — who released, who
   received, place, timestamp, identification, condition, both
   signatures. Highest value on the list: homes get sued over who had
   the body and when.
2. Vendor invoice generated from the job record — no re-entry.
3. Equipment service report / PM checklist.
4. Then whatever sit-downs actually ask for. Do not build the whole
   library up front.
Also: removal checklist, authorization to embalm, embalming case
worksheet, grave open/close work order (with the 811 ticket number),
vault set confirmation, monument setting, COI request.
**Hard rule for code review:** for cremation, Alabama's Board-issued
state identification form must accompany remains through transport,
cremation, and return, and establishments may not alter it or
substitute their own. The library **links** to the Board's form and
never replaces it.

**B15. Forms are a seat benefit.** Full set for comped and paying seats;
a vendor bidding free before their first win gets only what's needed to
complete a job they've been awarded. This is the answer to seat churn —
in a month with no awards, the seat buys the paper, not the matching.

**B16. Actual 1099 filing** — only after the CPA answers D5. If it lands
on you, turn on Stripe Connect Tax Reporting (create, file, deliver,
and adjust for transactions settled outside Stripe — which is exactly
your handshake jobs). Do not hand-build 1099 generation.

Fee logic unchanged and hard-coded: awards 1–10 in the corridor $0 and
still recorded; award 11+ 10% on top, min $15, cap $150; handshake only
after a first Pay-on-TradeBook charge, never refunding a fee.

## SECTION C — TERMS AND BURDEN OF PROOF

Replaces any earlier credential language. Burden sits entirely with the
vendor.

| Party | Carries |
|---|---|
| Vendor | Holding the license. Carrying the insurance. Producing proof on demand. Complying with state and federal law. All of it. |
| Home | Deciding whether it is satisfied before releasing remains or authorizing work. |
| TradeBook | Recording what the two of them did, with timestamps. Nothing else. |

Terms paragraph to ship (full text in `TRADEBOOK-CREDENTIALING-v2.md`):
vendors **represent and warrant** they hold every license, registration
and permit their state requires and carry appropriate insurance; the
burden of holding, maintaining and proving rests entirely with the
vendor; proof produced on request including at the job site, and a home
may decline the job if it isn't; TradeBook does not verify, confirm,
endorse or guarantee anything and performs no credential check of any
kind; credential information shown is supplied by the vendor or reported
by a home, recorded and not adopted; TradeBook is not a funeral
establishment, does not supervise, takes no custody; vendors indemnify
TradeBook.

Logging is not vetting. Holding a record of the home's check no more
makes TradeBook the verifier than a receipt makes the printer the buyer.

This posture is *safer* than the verified model — you can't be liable
for checking badly if you never claimed to check.

## SECTION D — BLOCKED PENDING OUTSIDE ANSWERS

These are phone calls, not code. Ordered.

**D1. Alabama Board of Funeral Services — the open one.** Must a
removal-only company with no prep room hold its own license, or may it
operate as a contractor of the licensed establishment holding custody?
Alabama regulates funeral establishments and "mortuary services" (a
location where embalming or cremation is practiced for a licensed
establishment, requiring a prep room and a properly licensed vehicle),
but does not appear to publish a standalone removal-service registration
the way Tennessee does. Both readings are plausible from the statute.
**Get it in writing. Your largest category depends on it.**

**D2. Attorney, short consult.** Is the Section C disclaimer clear
enough, and does the on-site log hold up as a record? Cheaper question
than the verification model would have been.

**D3. Georgia board** — removal credential, before any GA city.
**D4. Local AHJ** — gas and electrical scope on retort and lift work.

**D5. CPA — before the first live charge, not in December.** Stripe
issues 1099-Ks where Stripe controls pricing or the connected account
pays Stripe's fees directly; where the **platform** controls pricing,
the platform is responsible for filing. TradeBook sets the fee, so on
the current design the filing duty is probably yours — and that follows
from the Connect account type you pick. Three questions: (1) which
Connect type puts the filing where I want it? (2) am I a payment
settlement entity for these transactions, or is Stripe? (3) do I have
any reporting duty for handshake settlements I recorded but never
processed? Cheaper to answer now than to unwind in January.

Tax rules confirmed this session, no call needed:
- Card and third-party-network payments are reported on Form 1099-K by
  the payment settlement entity under §6050W and are **not** subject to
  1099-NEC or 1099-MISC reporting by the payer (IRS instructions for
  1099-MISC/NEC).
- 1099-NEC threshold rose from $600 to **$2,000** for payments made
  starting in 2026 (OBBBA §70433, P.L. 119-21, signed 4 July 2025),
  inflation-indexed from 2027. At a ~$375 ticket that's six direct jobs
  with one vendor.
- 1099-K third-party-network threshold reverted to more than **$20,000
  and more than 200 transactions**, retroactively (IR-2025-107, Fact
  Sheet 2025-08). Payment-card transactions have **no minimum**.

Research already settled, no call needed:
- AL licenses embalmers and cremationists individually; 8 hours CE
  biennially including ethics and bloodborne pathogens.
- Cremation: Board's state ID form must accompany remains through
  transport, cremation, and return; establishments may not alter it.
- Removal permit from the local registrar required to move a body out of
  the registration district (§ 22-19-3); out-of-state transport requires
  embalming or cremation (§ 22-19-2).
- Cemetery/vault work: no practitioner license. Real requirements are
  GL, workers' comp, equipment coverage, the additional-insured
  endorsement cemeteries already demand, OSHA 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P
  (protective systems at 5 feet — grave depth), and 811 before digging.
- Endowment-care cemeteries and preneed moved to the Alabama Board of
  Funeral Services under the 2023 Act.
- OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1030 covers removal techs, not just prep-room staff —
  "source individual" expressly includes human remains. Training at hire
  and annually, written exposure control plan, hep B offered at no cost.
- Tennessee has an explicit **Removal Service Registration** via
  core.tn.gov, separate from director/embalmer/establishment licensure.
  Hard requirement if you ever go north.
