# PART 1 — CATEGORY PARITY (PLAN CHANGE)

v1 said: *"Vault / retort / cooler / grounds wait until removals award
weekly."* That is reversed. Cemeteries and vault companies are peer
categories from day one.

The reason isn't fairness, it's supply and demand mechanics:

- **A cemetery is a buyer and a seller.** It posts work (grave opening
  and closing, vault setting, tent and chair setup, monument setting,
  lowering-device and equipment repair, grounds) *and* it fills work
  that funeral homes post. A single cemetery account is two-sided
  density in one signature. No funeral home is.
- **Vault companies already run the routes.** They deliver, set, and
  often open and close. They are in the same cemeteries as the homes
  every week and they know which crews show up.
- **The categories are not seasonal in the same way.** Removals spike at
  night. Cemetery work happens in daylight on a schedule and is
  *plannable*, which means it can fill the board on weeks when overflow
  removals don't come. A book with only 2 a.m. work looks dead at noon.
- **Equipment repair is the wedge into cemeteries specifically** —
  lowering devices, tents, greens, vault trucks. That is DCR's room.

Revised category list, all live at launch:

| Category | Posts work | Fills work |
|---|---|---|
| Funeral home | ✔ primary | rarely (trade prep for another home) |
| Cemetery | ✔ primary | ✔ open/close, setting, grounds |
| Removal / transport | — | ✔ |
| Trade embalmer / prep | — | ✔ |
| Vault company | ✔ occasionally | ✔ supply, delivery, setting, open/close |
| Crematory / trade cremation | ✔ occasionally | ✔ |
| Equipment tech | — | ✔ (TJ's slot, disclosed) |

Liquidity sequencing still favors removals *for the pitch*, because
that's the pain a director feels at 2 a.m. But the roster no longer
holds cemetery and vault seats back, and the founding 25 is open to
them from the start.

# PART 2 — WHAT EACH CATEGORY ACTUALLY NEEDS (RESEARCHED)

Focus is Alabama (tier 1). Tennessee and Georgia notes are for expansion.

## 2.1 Trade embalming — Alabama

Regulated under Code of Alabama Title 34, Chapter 13; Alabama Board of
Funeral Services; Admin Code Chapter 395.

Minimum:
- **Individual embalmer license** issued by the Alabama Board. Embalming
  is a licensed practice; there is no unlicensed path.
- **A licensed location.** Embalming happens in a licensed funeral
  establishment or a licensed **mortuary service** — Alabama defines a
  mortuary service as a location with a street address where embalming
  or cremation is practiced for a licensed funeral establishment, and
  requires it to maintain a prep room with nonporous floors and walls,
  operating embalming equipment, drainage and ventilation, and at least
  one properly licensed vehicle equipped to transport remains.
  If the trade embalmer works *in the home's own prep room*, the home's
  establishment license covers the location and only the individual
  license matters. Capture both fields; require at least one.
- **Continuing education**: 8 hours biennially including a board-approved
  ethics and bloodborne-pathogens/universal-precautions course. Not a
  gate to bid, but it is a fair thing to ask a vendor to attest to.

Collect: embalmer license number, license state, expiration; establishment
or mortuary service license number if they embalm at their own facility.

## 2.2 Removal / transport — Alabama  ← CONFIRM, this is the open one

This is the least clear category and it is your highest-volume one.

What is established:
- Alabama regulates funeral establishments and mortuary services, and a
  mortuary service must maintain at least one properly licensed vehicle
  equipped for transporting remains.
- A burial/removal permit from the local registrar is required to remove
  a body from the registration district (Ala. Code § 22-19-3), and it is
  unlawful to transport a body out of state unless embalmed or cremated
  (§ 22-19-2). Alabama no longer uses a burial-transit permit for
  routine in-state movement; a completed death certificate constitutes
  authorization for disposition.
- Alabama does **not** appear to publish a standalone "removal service
  registration" the way Tennessee does.

What is unresolved: **whether a removal-only company with no prep room
must hold a license of its own in Alabama, or whether it may operate as
a contractor of the licensed establishment that has custody.** Both
readings are plausible from the statute and the practical answer decides
who can bid on your largest category.

**Action: call the Alabama Board of Funeral Services and ask them
directly, in writing if possible.** Do not write terms from a plan
document, this one included. Until you have that answer:
- Collect the establishment / mortuary service license number **if the
  vendor holds one**, and mark the field optional-with-explanation.
- Require everything in the universal set below (insurance, entity, BBP).
- Put the vendor's own warranty in the terms: they represent that they
  are lawfully permitted to perform the work they bid.

## 2.3 Cremation — Alabama
- **Cremationist license** (individual) — Alabama licenses cremationists
  alongside funeral directors and embalmers, with the same 8-hour
  biennial CE including ethics and bloodborne pathogens.
- Crematory must satisfy the chapter's crematory requirements; cremation
  is arranged through a licensed funeral establishment (§ 34-13-121(b)).
- A state identification form created by the Board must accompany the
  remains through every phase of transportation, cremation, and return.
  Establishments may not alter it or substitute their own.

Collect: cremationist license number, crematory/establishment license
number, and — worth a checkbox — attestation that the Board's state ID
form travels with every case.

## 2.4 Cemetery services — grave opening/closing, vault setting, monuments
There is generally **no individual professional license** for this work.
The real requirements are insurance, safety, and utility law:

- **General liability**, typically $1M per occurrence in this trade, plus
  **workers' compensation** and **equipment coverage**. Excavation carries
  one of the highest workers' comp rate classifications there is.
- **Additional-insured endorsement.** Cemeteries commonly require any
  outside crew — funeral homes, monument companies, vault companies — to
  carry liability naming the cemetery as additional insured and to
  furnish the certificate annually. Build the field for it; it is
  already the industry's own norm.
- **OSHA excavation standard, 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P**: protective systems
  required for excavations 5 feet or deeper, which is the depth range
  of a grave. Competent-person requirement applies.
- **Call 811 before digging.** Underground utility damage prevention is a
  legal obligation in Alabama, not a courtesy.
- **Endowment-care cemeteries** are now regulated by the Alabama Board of
  Funeral Services — the Alabama Preneed Funeral and Cemetery Act of 2023
  moved preneed regulation from the Department of Insurance to the Board
  and transferred endowment-care cemetery regulation to it as well.
  If the cemetery entity holds a certificate or registration, capture it.

## 2.5 Vault companies
Same shape as 2.4 — no practitioner license, real exposure:
- GL, commercial auto (they run heavy trucks), workers' comp, equipment.
- **Preneed**: if they sell outer burial containers on a preneed basis,
  that is regulated. Alabama preneed sellers need a certificate of
  authority and preneed sales agents are registered. This does not touch
  at-need trade work on the book, but capture a preneed COA number when
  they have one, because homes ask.

## 2.6 Equipment repair (TJ's own category)
No practitioner license. Require GL and commercial auto. Two flags worth
a field: gas/burner work on retorts and electrical work on lifts and
lowering devices may pull in trade licensing depending on scope and
locality — CONFIRM locally before advertising retort burner work as a
bid category.

## 2.7 Universal set — every vendor, every category
- Legal business name and entity type; **W-9** (you need it for payouts
  and the year-end totals in the retention spec anyway).
- **Certificate of insurance**: carrier, policy number, coverage type,
  limits, expiration date. GL minimum, commercial auto where they drive,
  workers' comp where they have employees.
- **Bloodborne pathogens** — 29 CFR 1910.1030 requires training at initial
  assignment and annually thereafter, a written Exposure Control Plan,
  and hepatitis B vaccination offered at no cost, for any employee with
  reasonably anticipated exposure. OSHA's definition of "source
  individual" expressly includes human remains. This covers removal
  techs and embalmers, not just prep-room staff. Attestation + date of
  most recent training.
- Counties served, 24/7 availability yes/no, crew size.
- Signed vendor terms including the compliance warranty.

## 2.8 Expansion states
- **Tennessee**: has an explicit **Removal Service Registration** — a
  separate credential applied for through core.tn.gov, distinct from
  funeral director, embalmer, and establishment licensure, and it
  excludes licensed directors/embalmers/establishments and their
  employees, government agencies, and for-profit ambulance services.
  If you expand to Huntsville-north or Nashville, this is a hard field.
- **Georgia**: the State Board of Funeral Service licenses establishments,
  crematories and now organic human reduction facilities; a distinct
  removal-service credential did not surface in this pass. CONFIRM with
  the Georgia board before opening any Georgia city.

# PART 3 — ONBOARDING: ATTEST NOW, PROVE ON SITE

Revised at TJ's call. Specifics get worked out in person; the app
collects an attestation, not a document pile. The vendor checks the box,
and by checking it agrees to produce proof when they show up for the job.

This is the right trade at launch. A removal operator entering
credentials on a phone at 11 p.m. abandons a twelve-field form, and a
COI sitting in a database nobody reads was never the real protection —
the real protection is a director looking at paper in a driveway.

## The signup screen — one screen, under 90 seconds

**Business**
- Legal business name
- Contact name · phone · email
- Counties served
- Categories they'll bid (multi-select)
- 24/7 available? yes/no

**Credentials — checkboxes, not uploads**
- ☐ I hold all licenses my state requires for the categories I selected.
- ☐ I carry general liability insurance, and commercial auto for any
  work where I drive.
- ☐ My people have current bloodborne pathogens training where the work
  requires it.
- ☐ **I will provide proof of license and insurance on request, including
  at the job site, and I understand a home may decline the job if I
  can't.**
- ☐ I'll notify TradeBook if any of the above stops being true.

**One typed field, licensed categories only.** If they selected trade
embalming or cremation, ask for the license number — one line, no
upload, no expiration date, no verification step:

> `AL embalmer license #` ______

Keep this. It costs the vendor eight seconds, it's the first thing a
funeral director asks about an unfamiliar embalmer, and a wrong or blank
number is a signal you'd otherwise never see. For removals, leave the
field present but optional until the Board answers Part 4 item 1. For
cemetery, vault and equipment work there is no license field at all —
those categories are insurance-and-attestation only, which is what the
research in Part 2 actually supports.

**Signature**
Typed name + timestamp + IP on the vendor terms. That's the whole thing.

Status after signup: `Active`. They can bid and be awarded. No second
gate, no pending state, no badge that implies TradeBook checked anything.

## The part that makes this work: log the proof on the job

The attestation is only worth something if the proof step actually
happens and leaves a mark. Put it on the job, not in a filing cabinet.

On the job screen, visible to the home from award until completion:

> **Credentials** — Stateline attested to license and insurance at signup
> and agreed to show proof on site.
> `☐ Proof shown` `☐ Not asked` `☐ Could not produce`

One tap by the home. It writes to the job record and prints on the
receipt:

```
credential_check
  status        shown | not_asked | could_not_produce
  checked_by    home user name
  checked_at    timestamp
  note          optional free text
```

Three things this buys you:

1. **The home gets the record they actually need.** "We verified this
   vendor's license and insurance on site at 2:14 a.m., logged on the
   job" is a defensible sentence. A checkbox in a signup form from
   eight months ago is not.
2. **You get data without doing verification work.** After thirty jobs
   you know which vendors always produce paper and which are always
   "in the truck." That is worth more than a COI you filed once.
3. **`Could not produce` is the home's finding, not TradeBook's.** The
   flag is recorded and visible on the vendor's future bids as the
   homes' own reporting. TradeBook may, at its sole discretion, remove
   a vendor from a category — but that is a business decision about who
   gets to use the book, not a determination that anyone was
   unlicensed. Keep that distinction in the wording everywhere it
   appears. You never verified anything; the corridor reported what it
   saw, on the ground, where it counts.

## WHO CARRIES WHAT

State it this plainly in the terms, in the help screen, and in the
sit-down when a director asks:

| Party | Carries |
|---|---|
| **Vendor** | Holding the license. Carrying the insurance. Producing proof on demand. Complying with state and federal law. All of it, entirely. |
| **Home** | Deciding whether it is satisfied before releasing remains or authorizing work. Asking for the paper. |
| **TradeBook** | Recording what the two of them did, with timestamps. Nothing else. |

Logging is not vetting. TradeBook holding a record of the home's
on-site check no more makes TradeBook the verifier than a receipt makes
a printer the buyer. If any screen, email, or badge ever blurs that,
fix the screen.

## What the home sees on the bid card

Plain and honest, no verification implied:

> `Licensed & insured — self-attested · Shows proof on site · 4 jobs on TradeBook`

Tapping it explains, in one sentence, that vendors attest at signup and
agree to show proof at the job, and that TradeBook does not verify
credentials. Say it plainly. A home that discovers the badge meant less
than it looked like will never post again.

## Terms language — replaces the Part 2.7 version

> Vendors represent and warrant that they hold every license,
> registration, and permit their state requires for the work they bid,
> and that they carry insurance appropriate to it. The burden of
> holding, maintaining, and proving those credentials rests entirely
> with the vendor. Vendors agree to produce proof on request, including
> at the job site, and a home may decline the job if proof is not
> produced. TradeBook does not verify, confirm, endorse, or guarantee
> any vendor's licensing, insurance, or workmanship, and performs no
> background, credential, or fitness check of any kind. Any credential
> information shown on TradeBook is supplied by the vendor or reported
> by a home; TradeBook records it and does not adopt it. TradeBook is
> not a funeral establishment, does not supervise the work, and does
> not take custody of remains. Homes are responsible for satisfying
> themselves as to any vendor's credentials before releasing remains or
> authorizing work. Vendors indemnify TradeBook against claims arising
> from work they perform or credentials they misrepresent.

**This is the version to run.** Note that it is a different legal posture
than the verified model, and in one specific way a safer one: you are
not claiming to have checked anything, so you are not on the hook for
having checked it badly. That is worth saying to the attorney when you
have the consult — the question is no longer "what duty did verification
create" but "is the disclaimer clear enough and does the on-site log
hold up." Shorter consult, cheaper answer.

## If it ever needs to grow up

Don't build any of this now. But when a home asks for it, or when
`could_not_produce` starts showing up, the upgrade path is one step:
add an optional COI upload with an expiration date, and let vendors who
provide it show a stronger line on the bid card. Vendors will opt in on
their own once one of them is winning work partly on it.
