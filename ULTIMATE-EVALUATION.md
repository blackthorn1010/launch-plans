# EVALUATION — TRADEBOOK ULTIMATE LAUNCH PLAN
Reviewed 8 Sep 2026 against ULTIMATE.md (locked 8–9 Sep 2026).
Format: what holds · what breaks · what's missing · the week's fixes.

---

## WHAT HOLDS UP

**Density-first with a real kill date.** One metro, named gates, and
"stop talking" written into weeks 3 and 8. Most solo marketplace plans
have no falsifiable failure condition. This one does, in advance,
in writing. Keep it.

**Fee $0 on awards 1–10 but still recorded.** This is the strongest
single decision in the document. It buys the habit before it taxes it
and it does not teach anyone that off-book is the cheap lane. The
reasoning in "Why this mix" is correct: taxing job #1 is why a director
calls Joe instead.

**The sit-down script with explicit PASS/FAIL.** Ten minutes, fake job
said out loud, no card, one question at the end. The FAIL conditions
(they think cheapest won; winner sees a generic Awarded) are the two
real failure modes and you can only catch them by watching a human do
it. This is the most valuable page in the plan.

**Brand split.** Non-negotiable is the right register. Sealed bids and
founder-does-not-decide-his-own-dispute are the two rules that keep the
vendor side from deciding this is Standridge's lead desk.

**Overflow positioning.** "Use your guy — this is for the night he
doesn't pick up" is defensible, honest, and survives contact with a
skeptical director. It is also the only positioning that a competitor
with more capital cannot immediately copy without confusing their own
pitch.

## WHAT BREAKS

### 1. The plan measures repeat but does not build for it
30-day home repeat at 40%+ is a green metric. Nothing in the shipped
product earns a second post once the home has the vendor's number. The
matching screen is the entire product, and matching is exactly what
stops being valuable after award #1. **This is the largest gap and the
reason for the companion retention spec.** Fix: same-vendor re-post,
job record + receipt, change orders. Ship the first two before Huntsville.

### 2. "Vendors before homes" is stated, then contradicted by the roster
Phase 1 says slip a home sit-down if there aren't 3 live removal vendors
by day 10. But:
- First-10 list = 6 homes, 2 cemeteries, 1 removal (unconfirmed), 1 blank.
- Liquidity roster slots 3–6 (removal) and 7–10 (embalmers) are OPEN with
  no names and no sourcing method.
- Phase 1's named order runs four homes deep before a single vendor.

So the day-10 vendor gate has no plan attached to it and will fire.
If Stateline says no or has stopped running removals, Phase 1 has exactly
one live vendor — you — and the liquidity pitch (removals) has zero supply
while every sit-down promises it. **This is the most likely way the whole
launch stalls, and it is a sourcing problem you can solve this week
with a phone.**

Fix: before sit-down #1, name 6 corridor removal candidates the same way
the homes are named — firm, person, phone, city. Sources you already
have: who DCR sees in prep rooms, who the first-10 homes currently call
(ask that question *in* the sit-down: "who do you use now when you farm
one out?" — that is a supply-sourcing question disguised as rapport),
and the AL/TN transport operators you know from repair calls. Gravitas
(Grovetown GA) is out of corridor and does not count.

### 3. Two of ten sit-down slots are for a category you decided not to launch
Line 18: vault / retort / cooler / grounds wait until removals award
weekly. Line 141: Elmwood is Dignity, script only, not sit-down #1.
Yet the First 10 lists Elmwood at #7 and Jefferson Memorial at #8, and
#2 and #8 share Currie — the doc admits that double-count itself.

Net real independent density in the first ten is about six firms.
Fix: move both cemeteries to the notify-when-live list, replace with two
more corridor independents or two removal vendors. Run nine, or run a
different ten.

### 4. Recuse-or-publish is due now, not "before the first equipment award"
Phase 1 *is* an equipment award: the one-sided tool is a home posting a
down Portiboy and TJ bidding it. That happens in week 1 or 2. Recusing
for 90 days deletes Phase 1 entirely, so the decision is effectively
already made — publish it.

Fix: write the one-paragraph disclosure now, put it on the vendor
signup screen and in the terms, and pair it with the two rules that make
it survivable: sealed bids enforced server-side, and a named third party
(another director, not you) who decides any dispute on a job you bid.
An undisclosed founder-vendor discovered later by the founding 25 is
unrecoverable. Disclosed up front it is normal.

### 5. The week-3 kill gate may fire on a plan that is working
The gate: zero real jobs posted by anyone but you by week 3.
The math against it: 2 sit-downs/week cap → about 4–6 comped homes by
week 3. A 113-case home that farms out ~25% has roughly 2 outside jobs
a month. That is maybe 8–12 total opportunities, each occurring at
2 a.m. under stress, with a phone habit twenty years deep and an app
they touched once. Zero real posts by week 3 is a plausible outcome
for a product that is fine.

The gate is diagnosing the wrong thing: it will read a *recall* failure
as a *product* failure.

Fix, two parts:
- Instrument the denominator. In every sit-down, get the commitment:
  "text me when you next farm one out, even if you don't use the book."
  Then you know whether the misses are 0-for-2 or 0-for-11, and whether
  they forgot or chose not to.
- Give recall a crutch: SMS-to-post (spec §4). If they can text the job
  to your line and tap one link, the gate finally measures the product.
- Keep week 3 as a *review* trigger and let week 8's award count stay the
  hard kill. Week 8 is well constructed; week 3 as written is a coin flip.

### 6. Vendor vetting is absent, and it is the first question a home asks
The plan places vendors who transport human remains, and there is no
insurance requirement, no licensing check, and no vetting standard
anywhere in the document. The first thing a funeral director asks about
an unfamiliar removal service is whether they're insured and licensed —
and the first bad no-show or mishandled transfer in a corridor this
small becomes the story that follows the brand.

Fix before the first non-TJ removal award:
- Vendor onboarding requires proof of general liability insurance and,
  for transport, whatever Alabama actually requires for a removal
  service to operate. **Confirm the current AL licensing requirement
  with the Alabama Board of Funeral Service before you write terms —
  I'm not a lawyer and this is exactly the kind of thing that varies
  by state and by whether the operator is affiliated with a licensed
  establishment.** TN and GA differ; that matters at corridor expansion.
- Display it as a badge on the bid: `Insured · verified 9/26`. That is
  another thing a phone call cannot show and it strengthens the book on
  job two.
- Terms already disclaim workmanship. Verification is not a guarantee —
  say so plainly and still verify.

### 7. Nobody is ever asked to pay before the Phase 3 decision
First-10 homes comped 90 days. Vendors free until first win or 90 days.
So the first invoice lands around week 13 — after the week-12 living-book
call. You will decide whether to continue on award volume alone, with
zero evidence anyone will pay $29.

Fix: add `first paid seat` as a dated scoreboard event and pull one
conversion forward. Around week 8, ask one home who has actually used
the book to convert early at the annual rate. One yes is worth more
signal than ten more comps. One no, with the reason, is worth even more.

### 8. Leakage is not on the Monday scoreboard
The scoreboard tracks award health but not whether the book is being
used around. Add three lines from the retention spec: repeat-pair share,
handshake vs Pay-on-TradeBook share, same-vendor re-post rate. Standing
rule already written and worth keeping: if handshake runs past 40% after
award #10, add record value — do not raise the fee.

## SMALLER NOTES

- **Time split.** 20% to Death Care Repair, which is stated to be the
  living. If DCR revenue dips, TradeBook's runway dips with it. Worth
  a written floor: the minimum DCR weeks per month you will not cut into.
- **#10 blank (trade embalmer).** The plan says fill or run nine. Running
  nine is correct — do not invent a name. But trade embalming is 15–20%
  of the expected mix, so an unnamed embalmer means slots 7–10 of the
  liquidity roster are also empty. Same sourcing question as #2 above.
- **Priority +$29 hidden until 3 vendors in a specialty.** Correct, and
  it means the feature may not surface for a year. Make sure it isn't
  built ahead of that.
- **"Bid in 4 hours or I find you one myself."** This is the right
  promise and it is a personal SLA on a 24/7 window while you are also
  running repair calls. Decide now what happens when a 3 a.m. post gets
  no bid and you are under a coach with a hearse. Suggested honest
  version: bid in 4 hours during published hours; overnight posts get a
  bid by 8 a.m. or a phone call from you.
- **Google Search Console before any GBP** — correct sequencing, no note.
